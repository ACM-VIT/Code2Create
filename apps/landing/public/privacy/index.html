Privacy notice

Last updated September 2, 2026

This notice explains how ACM-VIT uses information submitted through the Code2Create website and participant portal.

What we collect

We collect your Google account identity, registration and contact details, eligibility information, team membership, project answers, and submission history.

During the event, we may also record attendance, hall location, support activity, and information from GitHub or Figma that your team chooses to connect.

The site also creates sign-in and security logs and records limited product analytics, including page visits and errors. Session recording and automatic click capture are disabled.

How we use it

We use this information to verify eligibility, manage teams, review applications, run the event, contact participants, provide support, keep people safe, and protect the platform.

Project text and AI

Project text may be sent to OpenAI for the live novelty comparison, follow-up questions, and a structured admission recommendation for organizers. Identity and contact details are excluded from these requests. Do not put personal data, passwords, or secrets in project answers.

AI does not admit or reject teams and does not set judging scores. OpenAI API data is not used to train its models. Provider safety logs may retain request content for up to 30 days unless stricter account controls apply.

Who can access it

Authorized ACM-VIT organizers and reviewers can access only the information needed for their work. Service providers process data for authentication, hosting, communication, integrations, and AI. We do not sell participant data.

How long we keep it

Retention depends on the record. We keep information while it is needed to run the event, handle decisions and support, investigate safety or security issues, and meet legal obligations. Access remains limited during that time.

Your choices

Required registration information is needed to participate. Contact ACM-VIT if you need to correct your information or ask about your data.
