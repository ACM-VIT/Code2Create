# Code2Create before 2025: what is worth bringing back

Research date: 5 September 2026. Prepared for the Code2Create 7.0 project. These are research findings and proposed product directions, not approved specification changes.

**The strongest opportunity is to make the venue itself feel interactive.** The older C2C projects supplied memorable shared moments: a puzzle winner interrupting the game screens, the room choosing its soundtrack, a quiz with pitch-order stakes, and digital coupons used at physical counters. The current platform already has substantial event operations. Its most valuable new layer would be an audience-facing stage view, a revived Intruder game, and a lasting showcase of what teams built.

**Scope and confidence.** I enumerated 355 repositories visible through the authenticated ACM-VIT organization API and identified 22 directly related older repositories by name, description, and source references. I inspected their source trees and important product flows, compared non-default branches, and read the significant unmerged 2022–23 implementations. Core snapshots are pinned to the latest reachable default-branch commit dated before 1 January 2025; recommendations do not use the 2025 C2C edition. For example, Octave is examined at its January 2021 snapshot, despite later repository activity.

This is source research, not a claim that every feature ran successfully at an event. I did not build the old applications or call their historical service endpoints. Vendored libraries, generated bundles, dependency update branches, and every historical commit were not reviewed line by line. Deleted repositories and repositories unavailable to this account are outside the inventory. No separate 2018 C2C repository was identified. The one directly identified 2024 C2C repository contains a template README rather than a working sponsor website.

**The unusual ideas, with implementation evidence**

1. **Intruder, 2019: solving a puzzle earned a room-wide interruption.** The README describes ten questions and a competition to cause the most intrusions. In the server, the first successful answer enters a shared pause, asks that player for a message, broadcasts it to other connected Intruder clients, and advances the question. There is a separate spectator/stats client, admin question controls, and a short message deadline. This is an application-controlled game effect on participating screens. [Event description](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/intruder/blob/7ff84e85f193f42d56b24f0db8f1c62d73102970/README.md); [socket implementation](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/intruder/blob/7ff84e85f193f42d56b24f0db8f1c62d73102970/server/src/controllers/socket.ts#L228); [spectator screen](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/intruder/blob/7ff84e85f193f42d56b24f0db8f1c62d73102970/client/src/components/dashboard/statsListener.jsx).

   **Our adaptation:** schedule a 10–15 minute Intruder session during a break. Teams answer on their phones; the fastest correct team earns a brief takeover of the venue display with its name, chosen phrase, and visual theme. Start with curated phrases, a small question set, a moderator start/pause control, and one server-awarded winner per question. Keep its score within the side event. This is the most distinctive feature to revive.

2. **2017: a GK/Googling quiz determined the final pitching order.** The participant FAQ explicitly says the quiz determined pitch order and that the lowest-ranked team would pitch first. This is a documented event rule; I did not find evidence here proving an automatic quiz-to-pitch-scheduler integration. The Android application does implement a 15-question card-based quiz drawn from a 30-question bank, with start, progress, and finish requests. [Rules and question bank](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/code-to-create/blob/a693cf521e2683c0b75ba4e91a9c8f92d99a680d/app/src/main/java/acm/event/codetocreate17/Model/Data/DataGenerator.java#L37); [quiz flow](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/code-to-create/blob/a693cf521e2683c0b75ba4e91a9c8f92d99a680d/app/src/main/java/acm/event/codetocreate17/View/Fragments/QuizFragment.java#L290).

   **Our adaptation:** preserve the anticipation and give quiz winners an entrance song, a spotlight slot, or a collectible badge. If pitch-slot choice is desired, make it an explicit event-policy decision with a fair fixed schedule. The first version should not make a side game control our existing review queues or judging.

3. **2017: teams on a “red list” got a chance to recover.** The FAQ describes putting the bottom 25 teams on a warning list after the first inspection, then eliminating those without improvement after the second. The schedule includes the announcement between inspections. These are documented rules and schedule copy, not verified records of actual eliminations. [Participant FAQ and timeline](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/code-to-create/blob/a693cf521e2683c0b75ba4e91a9c8f92d99a680d/app/src/main/java/acm/event/codetocreate17/Model/Data/DataGenerator.java#L37).

   **Our adaptation:** a private recovery plan: one concrete thing to demonstrate by the next checkpoint, an available mentor, and an acknowledgement when the team has addressed the blocker. We already have shared findings and next-checkpoint commitments in the current review code, so much of this is event procedure and better presentation. Avoid a public ranking of struggling teams.

4. **Octave, 2020: the crowd could influence real music playback.** The client lets attendees add songs and vote; the backend stores voters, orders unplayed tracks by votes, reads Spotify playback state, and contains a loop that appends a high-voted track near the end of the current song. It was more ambitious than a playlist suggestion form. The source also contains an unfinished duplicate-append TODO, so this establishes implemented automation rather than proven reliability. [Voting and queue](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/Octave/blob/46a01be6a0bd964a16c7deba3da1fff2f9b1ac4c/backend/routes/apiRoutes.js); [playback loop](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/Octave/blob/46a01be6a0bd964a16c7deba3da1fff2f9b1ac4c/backend/routes/spotifyRoutes.js#L230); [participant explanation](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/Octave/blob/46a01be6a0bd964a16c7deba3da1fff2f9b1ac4c/client/src/components/landing/LandingGrid.jsx#L19).

   **Our adaptation:** extend Synesthesia's audience experience: a large now-playing view, visible voting, and scheduled music moods such as opening, quiet build time, and the final hour. Music requests, votes, moderation, windows, and a YouTube host-player component already exist locally. Building another music backend would duplicate current work. The useful addition is the shared screen and event programming.

5. **2017: a complete digital-to-physical coupon workflow.** Participants had QR coupons for coffee, breakfast, lunch, snacks, and dinner. A separate organizer Android app scanned coupons, and the backend implemented redemption and reversal. A third app handled on-spot registration. [Participant coupon generation](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/code-to-create/blob/a693cf521e2683c0b75ba4e91a9c8f92d99a680d/app/src/main/java/acm/event/codetocreate17/View/Fragments/CouponsFragment.java#L77); [organizer scanner](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/code-to-create-admin/blob/a4fad63d0b566ae23e49558549bf8f8cdc01e5be/app/src/main/java/acm/event/code2createadmin/Fragments/ScannerFragment.java#L88); [coupon backend](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/Code2Create-AndroidApp/blob/8b95177492b2a7b547d7ef0081112c1a5ac71698/api/controllers/CouponController.js).

   **Our adaptation:** an event passport combining visible earned badges, sponsor activity stamps, and eligible physical perks. The existing Benefits module distributes shared or unique codes to people or teams; physical-counter redemption would be a separate capability to add. Start with one perk and one staffed counter. Meals remain ordinary entitlements, independent of optional games or sponsor activities.

6. **2019: projects were browsable beyond their judging forms.** A separate portal fetched submissions by eight categories, including DevOps, data integrity, construction, and accessibility. Its cards were structured around the problem, solution, challenges, technology, future work, repository, and team. There is a repository-link prop mismatch in the checked-in components, so the exact rendering should not be treated as polished. [Category browser](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/c2c-project-portal/blob/74f3a575589fc2b6f9c502d824c2b0c0db341563/src/components/projects/index.js); [project card](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/c2c-project-portal/blob/74f3a575589fc2b6f9c502d824c2b0c0db341563/src/components/projects/project.js).

   **Our adaptation:** a living demo wall during the event and a permanent C2C archive afterward. Teams publish a short description, screenshot or demo, approved repository link, and one milestone update. Rotate those cards on the stage screen. Team-approved public snapshots keep private proposals and reviewer notes out of the showcase. Audience favourites can be a separately named community recognition, not a judging score. This offers the longest-lasting benefit for participants and the chapter.

7. **2019: judging already had a structured progression.** The evaluation UI has technical inspection 1, technical inspection 2, and final evaluation. The first considers problem significance, understanding, and approach; the second considers implementation and completion; the final includes simplicity, design, practical value, technology, and product potential. The model stores inspection remarks and evaluator-indexed final records. [Evaluation interface](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/c2c_evaluation_portal/blob/38efc1fc7b311d8b5644b2b5a4039abaef4033dd/client/src/components/team_eval.js#L359); [team model](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/c2c_evaluation_portal/blob/38efc1fc7b311d8b5644b2b5a4039abaef4033dd/models/teams.go).

   **Our adaptation:** use this as historical support for coherent review-to-review feedback. The current project already has a richer review system; no parallel scoring product is needed. The explicit “Could it be a real product?” criterion is an interesting discussion point for the published rubric.

8. **2021: the public site and companion app had deliberate personality.** The site splits its large marquee into individual letters, changes their appearance under the pointer, and restores them after roughly a second. Its photo carousel uses scroll-linked reveals. The Android companion reads a live Firebase timeline, moves to the current event, handles notifications, and opens track PDFs. The website links both Android and iOS app listings, but no corresponding iOS source repository was identified in this inventory. [Interactive lettering](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/c2c-website-2021/blob/8c54fa0c43a928dd8d0d97fe99d398ba84469c72/src/Components/MarqueeSection/MarqueeSection.js); [carousel](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/c2c-website-2021/blob/8c54fa0c43a928dd8d0d97fe99d398ba84469c72/src/Components/CarouselSection/CarouselSection.js); [live timeline](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/c2c-android-2021/blob/78570ff7f93ec0d5d2075925b82344d6be8fb50b/app/src/main/java/com/acmvit/c2c2021/ui/timeline/TimelineFragment.kt#L42); [app links](https://github.com/ACM-VIT/c2c-website-2021/blob/8c54fa0c43a928dd8d0d97fe99d398ba84469c72/src/Components/DownloadAppSection/index.js).

   **Our adaptation:** a few discoverable interactions tied to the event—an optional clue hidden in the landing page, a reactive event title, a satisfying passport stamp. Apply these to the current design rather than adding a new visual system or requiring another participant app.

**Recommended build order**

| Order | Deliverable | Smallest useful version | Why it belongs here |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Venue stage view | One display showing current music, the next published event, and manually approved team spotlight cards | Immediately useful with the existing platform and music system. Establishes the shared surface for later features. |
| 2 | Intruder revival | One hosted session, a short curated question set, team answers, one winner per question, a brief approved screen takeover | Strongest historical identity and most memorable interaction. |
| 3 | Demo wall and archive | Team-approved project cards, optional demo media, rotating spotlight, persistent post-event pages | Gives participants something valuable to share after the event. |
| 4 | Event passport | One physical perk with a staff redemption flow; then optional sponsor/activity stamps | Adds the physical connection once the smallest counter workflow works. |
| Alongside reviews | Private recovery goals | Use existing findings and commitments with a named mentor and next-checkpoint follow-up | Useful event procedure; avoid inventing a parallel review system. |

These are relative priorities, not delivery-time estimates. A stage view and Intruder should be scoped as additions to the existing Vite/Convex applications. Reuse accepted event and music state, add narrowly scoped publication/game records, and keep the initial version independently usable. Implement game winner selection and physical redemption as transactional, idempotent Convex mutations. Project publications should be explicit records, so a public screen does not accidentally inherit private operational content.

**My recommendation:** make the shared venue screen plus Intruder the signature addition. Make the demo archive the durable addition. Enhance Synesthesia's presentation using the work already present. The historical lesson is that small, well-chosen interactions can give C2C its own personality without building several more disconnected applications.
